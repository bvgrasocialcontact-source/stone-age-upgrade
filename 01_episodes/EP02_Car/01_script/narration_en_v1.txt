=== ACT 1 ===
[excited] Three hundred thousand years ago, you wake up in the tall grass... and right next to you, there's a car.

Shiny. Red. Four doors. The logo on the front is covered in mud. [short pause] It will stay that way forever.

[mischievously] And by the end of this video, the whole planet will be built around this one car. [whispers] Even the ostriches.

But first... we have a few problems.

=== ACT 2 ===
[deadpan] Problem number one: fuel. A full tank gets you maybe six hundred kilometers. After that, it's a very heavy, very red rock.

Problem number two: roads. There are none. Just rocks, rivers, termite mounds... and the occasional angry rhino.

Problem number three: you have never seen a steering wheel. [whispers] You think it's a very round snake.

Problem number four: one flat tire, and it's over. Rubber tires won't exist for almost three hundred thousand years.

[excited] So... let's install an upgrade.

Version one point oh. A fuel tank that never runs out. Tires that fix themselves. And in the glove box... the owner's manual. Four hundred pages. [short pause] Mostly pictures.

That's it. No roads. No GPS. No driving license. And no, it can't fly. [mischievously] Yet.

Oh, and no. There are no dinosaurs. [short pause] We'll get to that.

=== ACT 3 ===
Day one. Your tribe, about thirty people, gathers around the shiny beast. The chief decides it's hungry. He offers it a gazelle leg. [short pause] The car does not eat the gazelle leg.

You climb inside. You touch everything. Then you press the middle of the round snake.

[laughs] HONK.

Thirty people are in a tree in under two seconds. Including the chief. Including the chief's grandmother.

Day two: the first drive in human history. You turn the key, the engine roars, and you move forward... at three kilometers an hour. [deadpan] The tribe walks next to you. Some of them are faster.

Then you find the other pedal.

[excited] Sixty kilometers an hour. Your hair flies back. The tribe screams. They think the beast is eating you. [short pause] Ninety seconds later, you have the first car accident in human history. [sighs] You hit the only tree for ten kilometers. The same tree the whole tribe was hiding in yesterday.

Day five. Something is running next to your window. Long legs. Big eyes. Feathers. [short pause] An ostrich.

You speed up. It speeds up. Sixty. Sixty-five. Seventy. [dramatic] The first race in history: a car against a dinosaur. Technically.

[deadpan] You hit a termite mound. The ostrich wins.

This one's real, by the way. Ostriches can sprint at about seventy kilometers an hour. They're the fastest birds on land.

Week two: hunting by car. Gazelles are fast, but they can't outrun a car. [short pause] They can, however, turn. [sighs] Cars can't turn like that. But the car is great at one thing: carrying home everything you hunt. In the trunk.

The downside? The engine is loud. The herds learn to run the moment they hear it. Soon the hunters have to walk twice as far to find anything at all.

Week three: the car becomes a house. Five seats. Thirty people. [laughs] Every night is a fight for the back seat. But on cold nights, there's the heater. Nobody freezes anymore.

And the cup holder? [awed] The tribe has never seen a cup. They stare at the little round hole for days. Then your cousin carves one out of wood. [proud] The first cup in human history. Invented to fit a car.

Month two. You open the glove box and find the manual. You can't read a single word. But you can read the pictures. A wheel. An axle. A wheel on an axle.

Your cousin builds a copy out of wood. [deadpan] The first one is square. [short pause] The second one is round. [excited] The wheel. Invented almost three hundred thousand years early.

In your world, the oldest wooden wheel ever found is about five thousand years old.

Month four: the first road trip. The next tribe lives a hundred kilometers away. On foot, that's four days. By car? Two hours. [laughs] They meet you, and they run up a tree. But this time, you bring gifts. Meat. Cups. One very loud honk.

By evening, the two tribes are trading. Stone tools for berries. Fire tricks for fishing tricks. [softly] For the first time, the people over the hill aren't strangers.

[excited] Year one. Thirty-four people. Distance driven: forty thousand kilometers. Trees hit: three. Races against the ostrich: twelve. Races won: zero. And you are now officially... "The One Who Rides the Roaring Beast."

=== ACT 4 ===
[excited] Okay. Let's speed this up.

Year ten. Everyone wants a car. There's only one. So the tribe builds copies: a wooden box on four round wheels. No engine. [short pause] Somebody has to pull it.

At first, people pull. Then someone has a terrible idea. [mischievously] The ostrich. Two ostriches on a rope, one wooden cart, and a very brave passenger. The first dinosaur-powered vehicle in history. [short pause] Technically.

Year fifty. The tracks the carts leave become paths. The paths become roads. And on the very first road... [sighs] the very first traffic jam. Forty ostrich carts and one confused mammoth.

The good part? Roads connect everything. Tribes trade, travel, marry each other. Words, songs and ideas move faster than ever. [serious] The bad part? Roads cut straight through the land. Herds stop crossing them. Some valleys go quiet.

Year one hundred. Too many carts. Too many crashes. So the elders invent the first traffic rule: the big red car always goes first. [short pause] Then they invent the first traffic light: a man named Ug, holding a red stone and a green leaf.

In your world, the first traffic light went up in London in eighteen sixty-eight. It ran on gas... and a few weeks later, it exploded. [deadpan] Ug never exploded. Ug is very proud of that.

Year two hundred: the first speeding ticket. It's carved on a forty-kilo stone slab. [laughs] You have to carry it home yourself. The fine: one gazelle.

In your world, the first speeding fine went to a man in England in eighteen ninety-six. His speed? Thirteen kilometers an hour. Four times the limit.

Year one thousand. The First Car now sits in a temple. [whispers] The engine has been running for a thousand years, because nobody knows how to turn it off. The honk is used as a prayer bell. And kids learn to read from the manual. [proud] The first sentence everybody can read: "Check tire pressure every month."

Year three thousand. To copy the engine, you need metal. So people learn to melt rocks: copper, then bronze, then iron. And they go looking for the black water that smells like the car. [serious] They find it. Oil. And with oil come the first oil wars.

Year five thousand. [dramatic] The first real car built by human hands. Wood. Bronze. A steam engine. Top speed: eight kilometers an hour. [deadpan] The ostrich is not impressed.

In your world, the first real car came from Karl Benz in eighteen eighty-six. And the first long road trip? His wife, Bertha. She took the car without asking, drove over a hundred kilometers... and bought fuel at a pharmacy. [laughs] That pharmacy is called the first gas station in history.

Year six thousand. Car companies! Mammoth Motors. Rockswagen. Fer-rock-i. Ostrich Martin. [mischievously] And the strangest cars ever built. A car with legs, for places without roads. A car that swims. A convertible... which was just a normal car, until a mammoth sat on it.

Year ten thousand. One car for every family. [serious] And the price shows up. Traffic jams that last three days. Smoke over every valley. Kids can't play in the street anymore. [softly] And crashes. Even in your world, roads take about one point two million lives every year.

Here, they did one thing early. On page one of the manual, there was a picture: a person, a belt, a click. [short pause] They read it three hundred thousand years early. Everybody buckles up.

[awed] Still, the good side is huge. Ambulances. Food trucks that reach villages during droughts. Cities that trade across whole continents.

Year twenty thousand. The traffic jam has lasted three days again. Somebody looks up and says the words that change everything: [whispers] "What if... up?"

[excited] Flying cars. In your world, people have been promising flying cars for about a hundred years. Here, they just built them. [sighs] Two weeks later: the first traffic jam in the sky. The birds are furious.

Year one hundred thousand. Cars drive themselves. A highway goes all the way around the planet. And the First Car? It's in a museum now. [short pause] Engine still running. Odometer stuck at nine million, nine hundred ninety-nine thousand, nine hundred ninety-nine. [whispers] The mud on the logo? Still there.

=== ACT 5 ===
[excited] So, fast forward to today. Two thousand twenty-six. Except here, they call it the year three hundred thousand, A.V. After Vroom.

Here's what's different from your world.

One. People here have been driving for almost three hundred thousand years. You've been driving for about one hundred and forty.

Two. Every city on Earth is built around one road: the Old Ostrich Road. The first road ever. It's a holiday route now.

Three. The biggest sport on the planet isn't football. It's ostrich racing. [laughs] And the ostrich still beats most cars off the line.

Four. Mammoths never went extinct. They pulled carts for fifty thousand years. Then cars took their jobs. Now they're retired, in giant nature parks.

Five. Every car on Earth still has a cup holder in exactly the same place as the First Car. Nobody remembers why. Tradition.

Six. The word for "hello" is a honk. [short pause] A short one. A long honk is very rude.

Seven. The longest traffic jam in history lasted forty-one years. [deadpan] People were born in it. People got married in it. Somebody wrote a whole book about it. In it.

Eight. Wheels are so old that nobody thinks of them as an invention. Asking "who invented the wheel?" here is like asking "who invented walking?"

Nine. Speaking of walking: it's an extreme sport now. [laughs] Walking ten kilometers gets you on the news.

Ten. Nobody calls it a steering wheel. Everyone still calls it the round snake.

[deadpan] Not everything is better, though. A third of the land is covered by roads. Wild animals live in special "road-free zones." And the air in some cities still smells like the black water.

[softly] And maybe the biggest difference of all: the first thing the car taught humanity wasn't speed. [short pause] It was that the people over the hill weren't monsters. They were neighbors.

=== ACT 6 ===
[warm] Back on day one, before the tribe went to sleep, the chief left one more gazelle leg next to the car. Just in case it got hungry in the night.

[softly] Three hundred thousand years later, it's still there. In the museum. Right next to the tire.

[mischievously] And the dinosaurs? Still none. Except the ostrich. [short pause] Which, in this timeline, has never lost a race. [whispers] Not one.

[curious] So here's my question for you: if you woke up three hundred thousand years ago next to a car, where would you drive first? Tell me in the comments. [short pause] The best answer might end up in a future episode.

[excited] Next time: you get a game console. No electricity. No TV. Twenty-nine people. One controller. [short pause] Subscribe, so you don't miss the next upgrade.
