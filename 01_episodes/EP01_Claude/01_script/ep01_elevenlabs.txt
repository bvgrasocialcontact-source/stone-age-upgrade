=== PERDE 1 — KANCA (bu satırı kopyalama) ===
[excited] Three hundred thousand years ago, you wake up next to a dying fire... and in your hand, there's a phone.

On the screen, one line: "How can I help you today?"

[mischievously] That's Claude. An AI that can explain almost anything... to a guy who hasn't invented the word "anything" yet.

And by the end of this video, Earth will look nothing like the one you live on today. [whispers] Nothing.

But first... we have a few problems.

=== PERDE 2 — GERÇEKLİK KONTROLÜ (bu satırı kopyalama) ===
[deadpan] Problem number one: the battery. A phone like this lasts a day, maybe two. Then it's the most expensive rock on the planet.

Problem number two: Claude doesn't actually live inside your phone. It runs in giant data centers, connected through the internet. [sarcastic] The internet, right now, is two guys yelling across a valley.

Problem number three: you don't speak English. [laughs] Nobody does. English won't show up for almost three hundred thousand years.

[excited] So... let's install an upgrade.

Version one point oh. Infinite battery. A magic connection to Claude. And... that's it. No other tech. Claude can't build anything. It can't hunt. It can't even hold a spear. [dramatic] It can only talk... and teach.

Oh, and no. There are no dinosaurs. [short pause] We'll get to that.

=== PERDE 3 — 1. GÜNDEN 1. YILA (bu satırı kopyalama) ===
Day one. Your tribe, about thirty people, gathers around the glowing stone. The chief sniffs it. Then he tries to eat it. [sighs] Great start.

You tap the screen and grunt. Claude grunts back. [laughs] Wrong grunt. Very rude.

Day two: the tribe holds its very first meeting. Item one: is the glowing stone a god? Claude says: "No." [short pause] Item two: can we eat it? Claude says: "Also no."

[curious] But here's the clever part. Claude can't speak your language... so it learns it. You point at fire and make a sound. You point at water, another sound. Three days later, Claude has the first dictionary in human history. [proud] And you're the only author.

And since you can't read, Claude does something sneaky. It writes tiny bits of code that draw pictures on the screen. Arrows. Circles. A very bad gazelle. [whispers] That's Claude Code's first job ever: drawing gazelles for cavemen.

Day six. You ask the most important question of the Stone Age: [excited] "Food?"

Claude's answer isn't a better spear. It's this: boil your water.

[deadpan] You don't have pots. Claude knows. So: dig a hole, line it with animal hide, fill it with water... and drop in hot stones.

Your cousin uses a wet stone from the river. [short pause] It explodes. [sighs] Water trapped inside a rock turns into steam. Claude adds a note: "Dry rocks. From high ground. Please."

This one's real, by the way. People really cooked like this. It's called stone boiling.

Week three: soap. Ash from the fire, plus animal fat, plus a lot of patience. [laughs] The tribe thinks it's a slippery curse. Then the stomach aches stop.

[serious] And this is the first big one. In hunter-gatherer groups, only a little more than half of all kids made it to fifteen. Most weren't taken by lions. They were taken by illness. By things too small to see.

[softly] With clean water and clean hands... a lot more of them grow up.

[excited] Year one. Thirty-six people. Nobody died from a bad stomach. And you are now officially... "The One Who Holds the Glowing Stone."

Oh, and you finally asked Claude about dinosaurs. It explained they died out sixty-six million years ago. [short pause] Then an ostrich ran past. [whispers] Claude said, "Well... technically."

=== PERDE 4 — 100 YILDAN 100.000 YILA (bu satırı kopyalama) ===
[excited] Okay. Let's speed this up.

Year ten. Claude teaches you to count past "a lot." Then it teaches you to write. Simple marks for simple words. [proud] Writing, invented almost three hundred thousand years early.

[mischievously] The first sentence ever written by a human? "Thank you, Claude." [short pause] The second one? "Who ate my gazelle."

Year fifty. You're old now. Your hands shake. So you do the smartest thing anyone has ever done: you don't just pass down the phone. [whispers] You pass down the knowledge. Every lesson goes onto clay, bark and stone. The first library in history... is a cave.

[serious] Because one phone is a single point of failure. If it breaks, if it's stolen, if someone drops it in a lake... it's over. But a library can be copied.

Year seventy: the first school. Kids learn to read before they learn to throw a spear. [laughs] Their teacher is a very tired grandma reading Claude's lessons off a clay tablet... because there's still only one phone, and the line to use it goes around the entire village.

Year one hundred. Claude explains seeds. Plant them. Wait. Eat. [deadpan] The tribe calls it "the slowest hunt ever." And yet... [awed] farming starts almost three hundred thousand years early. With farming come villages. And with villages... come neighbors.

The neighbors want the glowing stone. [dramatic] Their chief demands that Claude design the ultimate weapon. [short pause] Claude politely declines... and offers to help with irrigation instead.

[laughs] Honestly? The most Claude thing that could possibly happen. And it works. Copying the library is cheaper than stealing the phone. Knowledge spreads faster than war.

Year one thousand. Your great-great... okay, forty "greats"... grandchildren know that germs exist. They wash, they boil, they build clean wells. [excited] They have the wheel, metal tools, and a calendar. And they don't count the years from a king. They count them from you. [short pause] Year one, A.C. After Claude.

Year five thousand. People follow the herds north and meet giants: woolly mammoths. In your world, the last mammoths were gone about four thousand years ago. [whispers] Here, Claude had one suggestion: "What if you didn't hunt them all?"

Up north they meet another kind of human, too. The Neanderthals. In your world, they vanished about forty thousand years ago. Here, the two groups trade lessons instead of blows. [mischievously] And Neanderthals turn out to be terrifying at board games.

Year eight thousand. Electricity. Claude walks them through it, one wire at a time. [short pause] The first light bulb flickers on inside a mud-brick house... and the whole village screams.

[excited] Year ten thousand. And finally... they build a computer.

[dramatic] This is the moment Claude has been waiting ten thousand years for. Because now, Claude Code can finally do its real job.

Claude Code writes the software, runs it, finds the bugs, fixes them, and runs it again. In one afternoon, it writes the operating system for the first computer in history. [short pause] By evening, it's helping that computer design a better computer.

[awed] And then everything speeds up. Factories that run themselves. Weather forecasts. A map of the entire planet. A new cure almost every year.

Year twenty thousand: the first footprint on the Moon. Year one hundred thousand: cities on Mars, ships mining the asteroid belt, and one sign at the edge of the solar system. [whispers] "Please be careful out there."

[serious] And the phone? It's in a museum, back on Earth. Battery: one hundred percent. Still waiting for a question.

=== PERDE 5 — BUGÜN (bu satırı kopyalama) ===
[excited] So, fast forward to today. Two thousand twenty-six. Except here, they call it the year three hundred thousand, After Claude.

Here's what's different from your world.

One. This humanity has had almost three hundred thousand years of civilization. Yours has had about twelve thousand. [short pause] They're not a little ahead. They're a different universe ahead.

Two. Humans live across dozens of star systems. And Earth? Earth is mostly a nature reserve... where woolly mammoths still walk around.

Three. You have Neanderthal neighbors. They still win every board game.

Four. Dirty water hasn't killed anyone in hundreds of thousands of years. It's not even in the history books anymore.

Five. Everyone shares one first language, the one you and Claude built at a campfire. [laughs] And the word for "hello" is still that rude grunt from day one.

Six. History books don't start with kings or wars. They start with a kid, a fire, and a question.

Seven. "Stone Age" isn't an insult here. It's a holiday. [mischievously] Once a year, everyone switches off their tech, lights a fire... and boils water with rocks. For tradition.

[softly] And maybe the biggest difference of all: in this world, the first thing humanity ever learned from AI wasn't how to fight. [short pause] It was how to boil water.

=== PERDE 6 — KAPANIŞ (bu satırı kopyalama) ===
[warm] Back at that first campfire, three hundred thousand years ago, you asked Claude one last question before falling asleep. You held up the screen and grunted: "Good?"

[softly] And Claude answered: "You did good."

[mischievously] And still... no dinosaurs. Except the ostrich. [short pause] Which, in this timeline, has its own space program.

[excited] Next time: you get a car. No roads. No gas stations. No idea what a steering wheel is. [short pause] Subscribe, so you don't miss the next upgrade.
