Day two: the tribe holds its very first meeting. Item one: is the glowing stone a god? Claude says: "No." [short pause] Item two: can we eat it? Claude says: "Also no."

[curious] But here's the clever part. Claude can't speak your language... so it learns it. You point at fire and make a sound. You point at water, another sound. Three days later, Claude has the first dictionary in human history. [proud] And you're the only author.

And since you can't read, Claude does something sneaky. It writes tiny bits of code that draw pictures on the screen. Arrows. Circles. A very bad gazelle. [whispers] That's Claude Code's first job ever: drawing gazelles for cavemen.

Day six. You ask the most important question of the Stone Age: [excited] "Food?"

Claude's answer isn't a better spear. It's this: boil your water.

[deadpan] You don't have pots. Claude knows. So: dig a hole, line it with animal hide, fill it with water... and drop in hot stones.

Your cousin uses a wet stone from the river. [short pause] It explodes. [sighs] Water trapped inside a rock turns into steam. Claude adds a note: "Dry rocks. From high ground. Please."

This one's real, by the way. People really cooked like this. It's called stone boiling.

Week three: soap. Ash from the fire, plus animal fat, plus a lot of patience. [laughs] The tribe thinks it's a slippery curse. Then the stomach aches stop.

[serious] And this is the first big one. In hunter-gatherer groups, only a little more than half of all kids made it to fifteen. Most weren't taken by lions. They were taken by illness. By things too small to see.

[softly] With clean water and clean hands... a lot more of them grow up.

Month two. Your brother cuts his leg on a sharp rock. [serious] Back then, a cut like that could kill you. Claude can't stitch it. [short pause] But it knows a trick: clean the wound, then cover it with honey. Honey fights germs. People used it on wounds for thousands of years.

[deadpan] Getting the honey is a different story. Bees, it turns out, do not care about artificial intelligence. [laughs] Your face is swollen for a week. Your brother's leg heals perfectly.

Month four. The tribe asks Claude a hard question: how many gazelles are left out there? So Claude Code gets its second job ever. It writes a tiny counting program... and teaches you to carve one notch on a bone for every gazelle you see. [proud] Tally marks. In your world, the oldest known counting bone is a little over forty thousand years old. Here, you're about two hundred and fifty thousand years early.

[excited] Year one. Thirty-six people. Nobody died from a bad stomach. And you are now officially... "The One Who Holds the Glowing Stone."

Oh, and you finally asked Claude about dinosaurs. It explained they died out sixty-six million years ago. [short pause] Then an ostrich ran past. [whispers] Claude said, "Well... technically."
